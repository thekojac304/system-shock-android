SCALAR CURVE (8.8 internally; formulas below use shade-row units)

Input p = original permanent darkness, higher = darker:
  f(p) = p                         when p <= 4
         4 + 1.75 * (p - 4)         when p > 4
  ambient = min(15, f(p) + bias)
  result = clamp(ambient - strongest_pool, minimum, surface_cap)

Bias: floor 4.5; ordinary wall/object lower samples 6.5;
      every ceiling/upper-wall sample 10.
There is no compression above 8 and no universal ceiling cap of 12.
The ambient cap happens BEFORE pool subtraction: originally dark geometry can
still receive a substantial local lift. Contributions do not add together.

Away from pools, input p = 0,2,4,6,8,10,12,15 gives:
  accelerated f: 0,2,4,7.5,11,14.5,18,23.25
  wall lower:   6.5,8.5,10.5,14,15,15,15,15
  ceiling:      10,12,14,15,15,15,15,15
  floor:        4.5,6.5,8.5,12,13,13,13,13 (before path cap)
  object:       6.5,8.5,10.5,11,11,11,11,11

Caps/ranges: ordinary walls and non-exempt ceilings 0..15;
floors 0..13, with ramp/landing samples y=22..24 capped at 11;
objects 0..11. Sample coordinates x=26..27, y=20..21 have a minimum authored
row of 1 so bright warm-pool surfaces keep their amber bank identity.
Original transient light subtraction, camera lamp/muzzle, global modifiers and
rend3 full-bright behavior still follow this response and can override it.
Emissive textures 35 and 216 bypass BOTH scalar and bank modification.

POOLS: (x, y), Manhattan radius, lower/floor strength, ceiling strength
  display wall:   (30,24),   1.5, 13,10
  north bed edge: (28,21),   1.5, 10,4
  south bed edge: (28,24),   1.5, 10,4
  landing panels:(26,22.5), 1.5, 10,16
  supply ceiling:(26.5,20.5),2,  26,22
Weight w=max(0,1-ManhattanDistance/radius); lift=strength*w*w.
The implementation quantizes w to 8.8 before squaring. At half radius the
contribution is 25%, vs VIS1's 50% linear tent. Only the strongest pool wins.
The strength can exceed 15 because off-grid fixture centers are sampled at
tile corners. Supply-corner distance 1 with radius 2 gives a lower lift of 6.5,
not 26. These are interpolated corner lights, not per-pixel radial lights or
occluded illumination. No pool expands the original 17-tile mask.

COLOR BANKS / LIMITS

Five 16x256 banks (20 KiB total; +8 KiB over VIS1). Original palette unchanged.
Nearest-color search runs only at palette initialization, never per pixel.
The two original banks still change exactly 302 and 1271 of 4096 entries.
Final Horror cool/warm change 1559 and 1614 entries respectively.

For each original shaded RGB, Y=(2R+4G+B)/7:
  cool target = (0.30Y,0.85Y,1.25Y)
  warm target = (1.85Y,0.90Y,0.18Y)
Mix amount /128: cool row1=48, row2=88, rows3..15=128;
warm rows1..15=128. Original row0 is copied in every bank.
Source red dominance (R>2G and R>2B) or green dominance (G>2R and G>2B)
reduces amount by 4. Gold/orange (R>=G, R>2B, G>2B) reduces it by 8.
Inspected rose/skin indices 48..63 and skin/leather indices 128..159 reduce it
by another 4. This trades maximum grading for object/semantic color identity.
RGB targets clamp to 0..255; nearest available nonreserved palette index uses
weighted squared error 2*dR^2 + 4*dG^2 + 3*dB^2, as in VIS1.

All source indices 0..31, 255, invariant entries, and protected original outputs
remain unchanged. Ordinary pixels never map into reserved/cycling ramps.
The landing is now cool, and floor family 37 receives regional color in Horror.
Conservative retains its neutral landing/floor. Monitors and unlit objects retain
their existing exemptions; luminous panels retain their original blue/violet.

Observed palette constraints:
  The palette has no dense muted blue-green ramp. Many midtone gray/slate colors
  choose the same teal index or fall back to dark blue/purple. Stronger RGB targets
  cannot create missing hues, and increasing the grade merges texture colors.
  Original dark cyan output index 9 is protected by the reserved-output policy;
  for example source112 at row6 remains (0,27,27) even in the warm bank.
  Dark saturated blue source240 at row6 maps to near-black gray223 (11,11,11) in
  both Horror banks: blue detail is lost, not recovered by gamma.
  Gold source80 at row6 retains original index83 (95,71,0).
  Full-bright/reserved pixels and entire exempt fixture materials resist regional
  recoloring intentionally. Therefore the warm alcove has blue original fixtures
  over amber geometry, not physically consistent orange-emitting fixture art.
See dist/vis1b/evidence-summary.txt for actual final row cardinalities and probes.

KNOWN COSTS / READABILITY

Dark ceiling panels and rust marks deliberately merge/disappear. Walls can show
large stepped dark fields; the existing 16 rows and corner interpolation are
more visible with this contrast curve. Warm neutral/slate surfaces share orange
ramps and lose subtle original hue differences. This is the aggressive prototype
tradeoff, not an assertion that texture fidelity is preserved everywhere.
Doorway, ramp and gold control remain the navigation anchors. Objects retain an
11-row authored cap and skin/amber hue protection. Enemy visual acceptance still
requires an actual encounter; bank tests alone do not certify enemy visibility.
Existing unsupported translucent/voxel/invisible-critter and special-view paths
remain as documented in VIS1. No AI, map light, detection, lamp energy or save
data is changed by selecting a visual mode.

PROJECT GATE / RECOMMENDATION

Does the indexed renderer, aggressively pushed, transform the image enough to
justify the full horror-profile/auto-authoring direction?

My assessment: YES, conditionally on the user's visual acceptance. The actual
captures show a substantial change in shadow composition and simultaneous
warm/cool staging, not merely a modest tint. A has hostile dark recesses between
unchanged bright anchors; B has a clearly amber interior surrounded by colder
geometry. This is enough evidence to consider VIS2, rather than reject the
indexed renderer for lack of useful range.

It is not an unconditional recommendation to author the entire game. Decide
first whether the coarse bands, merged shadow texture, teal/blue quantization
and blue fixtures over amber surroundings are aesthetically acceptable. Enemy
readability and functional/special-light hardware cases also remain gates.
If those visible artifacts are unacceptable, more content authoring will not
repair the palette's missing intermediate hues; reconsider the indexed route.
No VIS2 system, external auto-authoring, larger mask or modern renderer feature
was implemented. Await the user's art-direction decision.

2-3 MINUTE ACCEPTANCE ROUTE

0:00 Load the existing fresh-start save (eighth/last load row), or New Game and
dismiss the initial help. Do not move. Pause -> Video. Cycle Original,
Conservative, Horror. Inspect the ceiling recesses, bright beds/panels, gold
control and route down the ramp. Leave gamma and device brightness fixed.

0:45 Return twice. Walk straight down the short ramp. In CURSOR mode double-tap
the gold wall button; the supply door opens beside it. Turn toward that doorway
and stop where both the Medical wall and alcove interior are visible.

1:15 Pause -> Video and repeat all three modes without moving. Judge the amber
interior against cold shadow, the original blue fixture, doorway silhouette and
floor. Return, approach supplies, inspect the pipe/pickups and gold control.

2:00 Toggle several more times. If desired, use CURSOR and the original fullscreen
HUD icon for a larger view; keep that same HUD mode for each comparison. Finish
in whichever mode you prefer. A nearby enemy encounter is a separate readability
check and may exceed this short acceptance route. No reinstall is needed.

The final visual decision belongs to the user. Do not automatically begin VIS2.
